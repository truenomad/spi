2
The conventional NPAFP indicator
The NPAFP rate compares reported cases with a population-based target.
NPAFP ≥ 3
non-polio AFP cases
per 100 000 children under 15 per year
Widely used and easy to interpret
Provides a fixed reporting target
Important limitations:
  • Uses a fixed target rather than each district's history
  • Does not account for seasonal or historical reporting patterns
  • Can miss districts reporting below their own expected level, including districts that previously reported many cases
  • Can meet the target even when reported cases are below historical expectations
3
The Surveillance Performance Index (SPI)
SPI compares the observed number of non-polio AFP cases with the number expected for each district, based on its own characteristics (e.g. population, season, history and neighbours).
Ratio =
Observed non-polio AFP cases
Expected non-polio AFP cases
By default, SPI = district ratio ÷ national ratio. With centre = "none", SPI is the district ratio alone.
SPI < 1
District ratio below national ratio (default)
SPI = 1
District ratio equals national ratio
SPI > 1
District ratio above national ratio (default)
Use SPI alongside the NPAFP rate to identify districts reporting fewer cases than expected, including those that meet the NPAFP target.
With national centring, SPI 0.95 means the district ratio is 5% below the national ratio; SPI 0.70 means it is 30% below. SPI = 1 is the reference point. Lower values may focus review; they are not validated action thresholds.

7
How to interpret STEPS concerns
Strength, extent, and persistence determine the review label. Timeliness and stool adequacy are reported separately.
Key points
  • Extent checks for low SPI elsewhere in the region.
  • Persistence checks for low SPI in the previous year.
  • Timeliness and stool adequacy describe other aspects of surveillance. They do not change the generated review label.
  • No single component establishes surveillance failure.
What a single concern means
  • Extent adds context from other districts in the area.
  • Consider persistence, timeliness, and stool adequacy together.
  • There is no requirement for agreement across all STEPS components.
How to prioritise
  • Consider SPI < 1 for review.
  • Larger shortfalls, similar findings in other districts, and shortfalls in the previous year support review. Also examine timeliness and stool adequacy.
  • Use a lower SPI cutoff (0.80, 0.60, or 0.40) to focus review on larger shortfalls; record the choice.
  • Helps decide where to review first. It does not forecast virus circulation.
8
Using the findings
1. Review
Use NPAFP and SPI to identify districts for review.
2. Assess
Use STEPS to assess the shortfall and related findings.
3. Respond
Choose actions based on the findings (e.g. supportive supervision, specimen transport, investigation quality, community engagement).
9
Key messages
NPAFP and SPI provide complementary information.
STEPS reviews five aspects of surveillance.
Extent checks for low SPI elsewhere in the region. Persistence checks the previous year.
Timeliness and stool adequacy describe other aspects of surveillance. They do not change the generated review label.
Use the full set of findings to prioritise review. STEPS does not produce a combined score.
